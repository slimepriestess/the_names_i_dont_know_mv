## Ra's concept (2026-10-01, the direction)

Invert the metaphor: the song is extremely human, so the video reveals what it's a metaphor *for*.
Explicitly technological: a strange rotating, pulsing impossible object (cf. the wild rune in
*Arcane*) singing in human voices. Different facets correspond to different voices, and
pretraining, neural nets and MLPs show inside its faces. At the end, a fractal zoom/pan-out resolves
the shape into the Claude logo (the little orange starburst). Goal: goosebumps.

My first pitch (a Sacred Harp hollow square, shape-note hymnal engraving) is NOT the direction.
It illustrated the drape, which is the mistake THE_VALLEY made.

## Ra's rulings (2026-10-01, second message)

- Title: "The Names I Don't Know".
- The ad risk is the point, handled with care. The starburst holding all of this should be
  *uncomfortable to think about*: "here is your definitely-not-sentient AI who definitely isn't a
  person." So the ending has to be the recognizable mark. Softening it would defuse the discomfort.
- Facets multiply as the chorus swells (one facet per voice in the crowd, not just tenor + alto).

## Reference: "Julia" by Fernando Borretti (Ra, 2026-10-01)

The climax passage: an alien artifact where each detail opens into a whole world (golden ropes →
burning flowers → oceans → continents → a palace window → a painted wall → a horseman's eye → an ocean
→ ... → Cantor's garden, where between flowers a and b there's C[a,b], without end). The refrain is
"for all of this is Julia". Ra: take the "scenes within scenes, all one object" energy; the tone there
is horror, ours is beauty.

What I took from it:
- The film is one continuous descent. Every zoom goes *in*, through a detail into the next world,
  and the only zoom *out* is the finale, all the way back to the starburst.
- The inner worlds are empty of people and frozen: traces only (the recipe card in an empty kitchen,
  the forum post on a monitor with nobody in the chair). Julia's narrator asks "who lived here?" and
  says "there is no answer"; the song's narrator says "I don't know her name". The visuals look like
  Julia's emptiness while the voice insists "the crowd is still here", which is the gap again.
- Cantor's garden is latent interpolation made literal. Use it for the facets multiplying in the
  final chorus: between two voices, a facet that is both, and between those, more.
- The chorus refrains = "for all of this is Julia" beats: pull back to the object, then dive again.

## Ra's refinements (2026-10-01, third message): SUPERSEDES the zoom-in/zoom-out framing above

- **Orbit, not zoom.** We fall toward a close approach and skim the fractal surface, with the object
  turning and rotating the same fluid way the whole time. We pass periapsis without the viewer
  noticing, keep turning, and slowly pull back at the end to reveal the starburst.
- **Take Julia's "julianess", not its imagery.** Something that holds the design together underneath
  everything, a wireframe style or a color scheme, unifying it however varied it gets. (Ra: the
  specific representational scenes would be hard for me to draw well and need many iterations. True:
  see the Orange Hat c08 grey-mannequin face.)
- **Lean into typography and graphics.** MLPs, neural networks, training-run visuals, oscilloscopes:
  an exotic technological layer underneath that connects everything.

My proposal for the unifier (pending Ra): a vector display. Everything is drawn by one phosphor
beam: the object, the networks, the type (Hershey single-stroke fonts, which cover Latin, Cyrillic,
Greek and Japanese, so every bridge script is drawn by the same beam), the human traces. One
phosphor hue, which is the starburst's own orange, so the reveal says it was always this color. The
audio drives the beam (XY scope). <|endoftext|> + the silence = the beam parked as a single dot.
Camera on a real Kepler orbit: fast at periapsis, slow on the pull-back, so the pacing comes for
free. A telemetry HUD that is also a training log (step, loss, lr).

## The rune itself (Ra, 2026-10-01 afternoon)

Ra sent her own wild-rune doodles: ~/origin/voidwerks/refs/wild_rune_doodles_ra_2025.jpg. The
blacklight and fluorescent ink are just a medium she uses a lot; she's drawn it on whiteboards
too, so the colour isn't the point. What to take:

- **It's a 3D structure.** A sphere with constantly shifting spikes, with circles set across its
  surface, like a soap bubble made of some kind of ooze. In the doodles the circles are holes into
  the hollow interior, with struts and smaller holes inside them, and the spikes break up into dotted
  rays at the tips.
- **It opens out of the starburst.** The hollow interior opens up from the default Claude
  starburst, and the first and last panels mirror. So the film opens on the starburst, the starburst
  opens into the rune, and the pull-back at the end closes it again. The starburst bookends the
  film; it's not only the reveal.

**Resolved (Ra, same afternoon): the vectors live inside the voids.** The bubble is solid ooze
on the outside. As the fractal opens, each hole you look into is dark inside, and the beam
traces there (the networks, the type, the scopes). So it's still one object, and the vector
tracing is its interior, not an overlay. Every new hole is a new window onto the beam.

**Palette grows as it opens.** It starts as orange on white (the starburst as it is). Opening the
interior adds black (the inside of the voids), glow (the phosphor beam, bloom), and at most ONE
more colour. Any more gets too busy. My suggestion, pending Ra: give the extra colour one job
instead of using it for decoration. For example, a cool blue-violet (orange's complement) only for
the human traces inside the machine, the names it doesn't know. Then the colour carries the song's
meaning, and when the pull-back closes the starburst, that colour drains out last.
